Brendan Shrader

I am a Ph.D. student in the Quantitative Biosciences program at Georgia Tech, where I study epidemiology and infection dynamics.

My interests include mathematical epidemiology, microbial population dynamics, differential equations, stochastic processes, and multiscale modeling. I am particularly interested in how mechanisms operating at cellular or within-host scales shape population-level epidemiological dynamics and evolution.

About

I completed a B.S. in Mathematics at the University of Central Florida in 2025, with a minor in Computer Science. My undergraduate research introduced me to mathematical epidemiology and led to an honors thesis on disease endemicity, partial immunity, and post-infection mortality (see Publications).

I am currently a NSF Graduate Research Fellow and am coadvised by Profs. Rachel Kuske (School of Mathematics) and Sam Brown (School of Biology).

In my free time, I enjoy rock climbing, weightlifting, swimming, playing the piano, and photography.
